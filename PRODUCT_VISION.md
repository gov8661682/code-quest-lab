# Code Quest Lab - Product Vision

Status: provisional product direction, 2026-08-04

## Player promise

Code Quest Lab is a self-contained, offline-first fantasy action RPG for secondary-school students. A player creates a hero, learns movement and combat through short dungeon runs, earns permanent progression, and returns to Town to improve their build. The game should be enjoyable first, with subtle computational-thinking support available when it helps a player understand a challenge, build, or outcome.

The primary experience is designed for 10-30 minute landscape tablet sessions, with reliable pause, automatic saving, checkpoints, a useful session summary, and a clear stopping point. It must remain usable on a keyboard when one is available, but touch is the primary input.

## Audience and environment

Primary users are students approximately 13-17 years old using iPads, Android tablets, or school-managed devices. Internet access, external websites, accounts, and permissions may be restricted or unavailable. Version 1 collects no personal information, requires no login, and includes no social, chat, or user-generated-content features.

The presentation should feel sophisticated enough for teenagers rather than designed for young children. Fantasy combat may remain, but the game avoids graphic injury, gore, sexual content, strong profanity, gambling themes, shock horror, and manipulative purchasing. The provisional store position is a teen-accessible general game, not an automatic Kids-category claim; the owner must complete the final age-rating review.

## Game identity and learning layer

The product remains a genuine fantasy action role-playing game first. Learning is authentic but unobtrusive: skills can express commands or functions, ability combinations can reward sequencing, enemy behavior can reveal patterns, dungeon planning can reward decomposition, builds can demonstrate optimization, and optional explanations can connect cause-and-effect, conditions, loops, and debugging to what the player just did.

Two connected layers define the experience:

- Core Adventure: the full fantasy action game, enjoyable without formal lessons.
- Learning Support: optional library explanations, post-session strategy summaries, pattern/build challenges, and debugging-themed activities that stay on-device and never require classroom accounts or submissions.

Do not rename every fantasy feature with coding jargon, force quizzes, or make unsupported claims that the game teaches programming or improves academic results.

## Product principles

- Offline-first and private by default.
- Touch-first tablet usability and managed-device compatibility are core requirements.
- One coherent core loop is more valuable than unreleased menu promises.
- Local progress must be recoverable and backward compatible.
- Touch controls, safe areas, readable text, pause/resume, and reduced-motion options are release features, not polish extras.
- Monetisation must unlock content transparently and never sell power, random rewards, or pressure.
- Engagement should come from fun, mastery, fair progression, and natural stopping points rather than scarcity, streak pressure, waiting, or fear of missing out.
- Every shipped visual, sound, font, and dependency needs a documented source and commercial-use status.

## Version 1 success

A new player can launch the browser or native shell on a restricted tablet, create a hero, understand touch controls, complete a meaningful 10-30 minute session, pause or stop safely, resume after suspension, and see a clear summary and next suggestion. Over time, the player can complete the intended V1 dungeon path and ending. A parent or school can use the product without ads, chat, tracking, redirects, accounts, or unnecessary permissions. A reviewer can test the full game in a documented development mode without making a real purchase.

## Business direction

Free introduction plus one parent-gated, non-consumable full-game unlock. No ads, subscriptions, consumable currency, paid random rewards, or pay-to-win advantages in V1. Browser purchasing remains disabled or explicitly sandboxed; Apple and Google purchases are platform adapters around the same core entitlement.

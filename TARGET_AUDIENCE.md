# Code Quest Lab - Target Audience

Updated: 2026-08-04

## Primary audience

Secondary-school students approximately 13-17 years old. The game is intended to support problem-solving, planning, pattern recognition, sequencing, decomposition, optimization, resource management, cause-and-effect reasoning, and familiarity with keyboard/tablet controls through an enjoyable fantasy action RPG.

This is a product audience decision, not a legal age-rating or Kids-category determination. The owner must complete the platform age-rating questionnaires and decide the final store positioning.

## Device and environment assumptions

- iPads and Android tablets are the primary devices.
- Touch controls and landscape layouts are the primary interaction path.
- Keyboard/mouse input remains supported when available.
- Devices may be parent- or school-managed, locked down, muted, offline, or limited to approved sites/apps.
- External websites, browser redirects, accounts, email, social login, and unrestricted web access may be unavailable.
- Students may have 10-30 minutes per session and need to stop without losing progress.

## Experience needs

- Fast launch and clear first-session onboarding.
- Readable teen-appropriate presentation rather than young-child styling.
- Clear objectives, checkpoints, pause/resume, autosave, recovery, session summary, and a natural stopping point.
- Strong touch affordances, safe-area handling, landscape tablet layout, and usable controls without a keyboard.
- Optional Learning Support rather than forced quizzes or long lessons.
- No need to submit work, create a classroom account, or disclose personal information.

## Safety and content position

Fantasy combat may remain, but V1 avoids graphic injury, gore, sexual content, strong profanity, gambling themes, shock horror, manipulative monetisation, behavioral advertising, chat, and user-generated content. No feature should pressure a student to ask an adult for payment or punish them for stopping.

## Non-goals

Code Quest Lab is not a classroom management system, online coding course, social platform, diagnostic tool, academic-outcomes product, or device-management workaround. Marketing must not claim school approval, programming mastery, or improved grades without evidence and owner review.

Educational purpose

Thinking skills can live inside the adventure.

Code Quest Lab uses combat, routes, upgrades, and recovery to give players low-pressure practice with planning, patterns, sequencing, cause and effect, and debugging.

This is a game that supports computational thinking through play. It does not claim to teach programming, improve grades, replace instruction, or provide a clinical or school-approved outcome.

Patterns

Players notice enemy tells, room layouts, timing windows, and repeated consequences. Recognising a useful pattern helps a player choose what to try next.

Planning

Before a room or run, players weigh route choices, resources, equipment, and risk. The goal is a meaningful decision, not a worksheet.

Sequencing

Movement, attacks, skills, and recovery work as sequences. Players can test an order, observe the result, and adjust it.

Conditions and loops

Enemy states, cooldowns, repeated waves, and changing room conditions make “if this, then that” reasoning visible without requiring code syntax.

Decomposition

Large encounters become smaller goals: reach the next room, protect health, identify a threat, and decide when to stop or continue.

Debugging

A failed attempt is information. Optional after-session notes invite players to identify what happened, change one variable, and try again.

Player choice and accessibility

  • Learning Support is optional and never blocks the fantasy adventure.
  • There are no forced quizzes, long lessons, grades, leaderboards, or public profiles.
  • Play remains usable with audio muted; touch is primary and keyboard/mouse input is secondary where available.
  • Teachers and families can review the same public description without needing a login or sending student data.
